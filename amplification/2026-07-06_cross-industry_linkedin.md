Most prompt engineering advice fails PMs, BAs, and Scrum Masters because it optimizes for the wrong thing — technical depth instead of decision quality.

The fix is a structural separation most practitioners miss: thinking modes and quality constraints are not the same lever. Thinking modes shape *how* the AI reasons (Steelman, First Principles, Decision Intelligence). Quality constraints shape *how trustworthy* the output is (Verifiable Foundations, Calibrated Confidence, Auditable Logic Trails). Conflate them, and you get either rigorous triviality or creative hallucination.

I've published a role-specific prompt framework with recommended stacks: PMs get Steelman + Decision Intelligence + L99. BAs get Socratic + Critic + Pareto. Scrum Masters get Systems Thinking + Decision Intelligence. Each stack is composable — swap modes across roles for cross-functional scenarios.
